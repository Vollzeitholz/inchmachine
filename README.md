Inch Machine
Turn a knob to dial a value; the other row converts live. Push a knob (click, Enter or Space) to choose what its window shows, turn to pick, push again to confirm. Slide the lever under a row to set the last window that counts. The panel is drawn at 4:3 and scales to fit a tablet or a phone held sideways.


Behaviour spec (draft)
Layout. Both rows have five windows and end in the same Remainder column. Below them, one row in three parts: the Zero button and click switch, then the inch and millimetre readouts.
Turn adds or subtracts one unit of that window's field, with carry like an odometer. One detent or one mouse-wheel notch is one step.
Input side. Turning a knob in the other row makes that row the input and carries on from the value it was showing. The INPUT lamp in the glass marks it. Zero clears both rows.
Zero stays empty. A window with nothing in it shows no digit, only its unit.
Push enters setup for that window: the unit blinks, turning picks a unit or off, pushing again confirms. Setup also ends after 6 s without input.
Windows stay in order. Units always run large to small from left to right. If you pick a unit that clashes, the neighbours move over to make room; a window pushed past the smallest unit turns off.
The leftmost active window takes the overflow (with in first, 8′ reads 96). Every other window counts up to the next larger active unit.
The lever under each row marks the last window that counts. The value is rounded to that window, and Remainder says how far the shown value is off, for example too small by 0.24 mm. Windows past the lever are dimmed and their knobs do nothing.
Imperial always shows one whole number plus one fraction, and the fraction sits in the window of its denominator. With in · ½ · ¼ · ⅛ · 1/16, 1 3/4″ fills the first and third windows.
Input keeps the fraction where you dial it. Seven clicks on ⅛ read 7/8 in the ⅛ window. A finer knob moves the fraction to its window (7/8 plus 1/16 reads 15/16); a coarser knob adds in place and carries into the inch (7/8 plus ½ reads 1″ 3/8).
As a result, the fraction is fully reduced and lands in the coarsest window that can hold it: 12/16 reads 3/4 in the ¼ window.
